# Gameplay verification and balance notes

Verified October 15, 2026 with Godot 4.5.1 stable on Windows. These results describe the **finite authored campaign revision**, manual weapon defaults, and the per-ship upgrade tree. The earlier timed/random campaign measurements have been superseded.

## Authoritative runtime evidence

The existing complete game suite passed **285 checks with zero failures**. It was not rerun for the separate balance observations below.

| Evidence | Result | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Control, combat, finite-fleet and navigation contracts | 63 primitive checks passed | Manual defaults, ownership gates, asymmetric cannon counts, independent primary/secondary cooldowns, touch release/pause cleanup, swept hits, land cover, exact fleet deployment and delayed victory |
| All 50 campaign levels with all four base ships | 200 / 200 wins | Every authored fleet can be reached and defeated using normal steering/cannon input calls; damage immunity is enabled for this feasibility matrix |
| Opening missions with ordinary hull damage | 12 / 12 wins | Levels 1–3 completed with all four base ships, manual controls, and no immunity |
| Island navigation stress | All six enemy kinds passed | Each kind travels around a blocking island, stays outside land and inside bounds; shoreline safety margins permit outward escape |
| Endless Ocean | 20 simulated minutes completed | Active enemy/projectile/particle/pickup populations and geometry caches remain bounded |
| Daily Drift | Timed 90-second challenge passed | Daily procedural spawning remains separate from finite campaign rules |

Raw output: `tests/output/game-tests.json`; log: `tests/finite-full.log`. The full run took 60.51 seconds of desktop wall time. Logs/output are local verification artifacts, regenerated by the commands below.

The feasibility matrix uses base ship statistics and the runtime bot's calls to the same steering, primary fire and secondary fire methods exposed to players. Automatic primary assistance is false throughout. All 200 results were additionally checked to have **sunk ships = deployed ships = authored total**. Damage immunity deliberately isolates completion/navigation correctness from survivability; these 200 wins are not evidence of human difficulty or equal ship balance.

## Current campaign and controls

The campaign contains 50 finite levels across five biomes, with **142 named encounters and 526 authored ships**. Each level has explicit bounds, entry position/heading, islands, hazards, supplies, and ordered ship formations. The first fleet is deployed at entry; subsequent fleets wait until the previous fleet is cleared and then respect their authored two-second delay. A campaign win requires all authored ships and all encounters. There is no campaign timer win/loss, random reinforcement spawner, procedural island generation, random supply replenishment, or distance-based enemy despawning. Bosses occur once in their final authored encounter.

Player and enemy movement share swept circle-versus-island resolution and map bounds. Finite navigation uses a 32-unit AStar grid with 48-unit island clearance, alongside local steering and outward shoreline escape. Offscreen enemy arrows and the boundary line help players find surviving fleets. The bot plans around land and opportunistically seeks nearby repairs/shields; it does not plan evasions for every hostile projectile or damaging hazard.

Primary port/starboard cannons are manual by default. A legacy `auto_fire` preference cannot grant the paid capability. Each side has its own purchased cannon count and reload. Port and starboard grapeshot are separately purchased, separately fired, and independently reloaded: three slower, heavy projectiles in a fan. Their automatic crews unlock at ship ranks 17 and 18; automatic primary broadsides are the rank-20 capstone. All automation also requires its paid prerequisites. Touch/mouse releases are captured before UI consumption, and pausing/finishing releases held controls.

Each case uses a new isolated progression object and test-only save path. XP is set to the specified rank threshold, a 20,000-gold test purchasing budget is provided, and every rank-eligible upgrade is bought through the real purchase API. This creates the intended loadout with actual receipts and prices; it does not establish that a particular human playthrough will have earned that rank or purchasing budget. Raw observations, loadout details, damage taken, supplies, remaining ships, end-of-run hazard proximity, costs and assumptions are in `tests/output/balance-observations.json`; the log is `tests/balance-observations.log`.

These observations show that the sampled upgraded loadouts can survive those authored fleets under this control policy. They are not proof of human balance. The bot reads exact enemy positions, reacts every 0.05 simulated seconds and has no touch latency or learning curve. It can also make poor hazard decisions that a person would avoid. A single deterministic sample per ship/stage is not a win-rate study. Neither bot wins nor bot deaths alone justify changing combat values.

The current sample suggests two useful playtest questions: do purchased cannon/grapeshot upgrades feel rewarding without making later bosses too brief, and can a new player steer while timing both primary and auxiliary controls comfortably? Golden Gull's lower final hull in the stage-20/stage-50 samples deserves observation, rather than an automatic stat adjustment. Also verify that actual campaign earnings support the intended upgrade choices before each biome boss.

## Endless bounds and performance limits

The authoritative active, firing bot completed 1,200 simulated seconds at a 0.05-second step. One verified run observed:

| Entity / cache | Peak observed | Limit or policy |
|---|---:|---|
| Enemies | 14 | Configured endless cap 14; a timed boss replaces a rival at the cap |
| Cannonballs | 26 | 220 |
| Particles | 195 | 320 |
| Pickups | 68 | 70 |
| Island chunks | 61 | Distant chunks pruned after the cache grows past 100 |
| Nearby geometry groups | 29 | Distant groups pruned when the cache grows past 36 |

The bot sank 731 enemies in this run. These are observed peaks under one active-play simulation; the hard projectile/particle/pickup caps are separately enforced. Headless wall time measures CPU simulation and setup without ordinary rendering, audio latency, Android thermals, or real touch input. It is not a phone frame-rate or battery measurement. Device testing should still include a stationary/non-firing stress run, long active voyages, background/resume, safe areas, multi-touch, and audio behavior.

## Reproduction

Use Godot 4.5.1 from the project directory, with an explicit writable log path on Windows:

```powershell
godot --headless --path . --log-file tests/finite-full.log --script res://tests/test_game.gd
godot --headless --path . --log-file tests/balance-observations.log --script res://tests/test_balance.gd
```

The game suite supports arguments after `--`:

- `--quick`: seven campaign levels per ship plus the other contracts and a three-minute endless sample.
- `--contracts-only`: the 63 primitive control/combat/fleet/navigation checks.
- `--endless-only`: the twenty-minute endless and timed daily checks.
- `--matrix-only`: campaign feasibility only; optional `--level=10 --ship=ironclad` narrows a diagnostic run.

The separate balance script always runs the 16 damage-enabled upgraded observations. Setup, purchase, automation-gate or isolation failures produce a failing exit code. A simulated combat defeat is reported as an observation, not hidden or treated by itself as a software defect. Both scripts use their own test paths and remove temporary saves; neither records simulated voyages into the player's save.

The environment can print a Windows root-certificate-store warning while running the engine. These gameplay checks are offline. They do not validate Android touch comfort, signed-build behavior, OAuth, Google Play Games authentication, cloud conflict handling, or real leaderboard submission.
